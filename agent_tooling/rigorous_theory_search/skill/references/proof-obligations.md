# Proof obligations and global assembly

Use one exact claim per ledger entry. State its quantifiers, types, assumptions, scope, evidence, falsifier, and status. In the dependency graph, `from -> to` means `from depends on to`; the target's ancestors are precisely the transitive dependencies reachable by following outgoing edges. Keep the graph acyclic; represent a genuine recursive construction by a separate existence or fixed-point theorem instead of a proof cycle. Every release covers the target and this full transitive dependency closure. No terminal status permits a `CANDIDATE` or `LLM_SUPPORTED` dependency ancestor, and both complete statuses require every dependency ancestor to be `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED`. A standalone negative certificate should omit irrelevant affirmative-route edges and record only the verified lemmas on which the refutation actually depends.

Before proof, check nonempty typed domains, regularity, support, normalization, domination, integrability, boundary conditions, and well-defined maps, actions, quotients, sections, pullbacks, and measures. Justify analytic interchanges such as differentiation under an integral, limit exchange, Fubini or Tonelli use, and variational differentiation. Check symmetry and the declared equivalence relation rather than equality in a chosen representative.

Every closable claim kind has an explicit evidence rule and the evidence record's `supports` boolean must match the closure direction. Mathematical verification requires direct `DERIVATION`, `FORMAL_PROOF`, or `APPLICABLE_THEOREM` evidence; refutation requires a direct `COUNTEREXAMPLE` or `NONEXISTENCE_PROOF`. A physical interpretation requires a declared modeling or operational bridge plus direct mathematical, primary-source, or numerical evidence. A modeling postulate requires mathematical or primary-source evidence of the scoped assertion. An operational identification requires mathematical, primary-source, or numerical evidence. A numerical observation requires `NUMERICAL` or `SYMBOLIC_CHECK` evidence. Refuting the last four kinds uses the corresponding direct eligible kind with `supports: false`, with counterexamples and nonexistence proofs also eligible where applicable. Numerical tests, finite enumeration, symbolic simplification without side conditions, figures, and agent agreement cannot close a mathematical claim. An applicable theorem needs a primary source, the exact cited statement, and a hypothesis-by-hypothesis mapping to the frozen contract.

One valid counterexample refutes a universal claim. Failure to find a witness does not refute existence. Negative closure for an existential target needs a scope-matched nonexistence proof. A mixed target follows its explicit frozen `negative_certificate_kind`, which must agree with the target's actual quantifier structure. Positive closure for an existential target needs a typed witness and a proof that it meets every requirement; examples do not close a universal target.

For a compound target, atomize each conjunct before certification. A counterexample may refute the requested conjunction, but it does not discharge the obligation to classify each conjunct, type every requested construction, or state the strongest valid conditional theorem. The released result gives a disposition for each conjunct and, for every failed identification or construction, names its missing assumptions. Do not use early negative closure to omit independent regularity, compatibility, bridge, or interpretation obligations in the frozen target.

Formalization is evidence only for the encoded statement under the encoded axioms. Record the theorem statement, assumptions, trusted kernel or checker, source artifact, and the mapping back to the human claim. A derivation must expose load-bearing steps and side conditions so an independent reconstruction can reproduce it.

Global assembly checks that local objects occupy compatible spaces, overlap maps and cocycles satisfy coherence, and uniqueness modulo the stated equivalence is proved. Write the full extended-valued functional and include all boundary, Jacobian, interaction, anomaly, entropy, normalization, and correction terms. A physical or ontological interpretation requires declared bridge premises and direct eligible evidence; theorem status does not transfer through analogy.
