## Audit remediation

New tests are in `tests/test_audit_20261012_*.py`.

**Model and theory.** The moment-matched floor of both predictive readouts is now `f * Sigma_p`, the token's local prior covariance (the layer prior, which is the embedding prior under `prior_handoff_sigma = 0`), added in local coordinates by `RelationalOperators.floor_term` (item 4, T5). It involves no frame, so a common reference change by a non-orthogonal `a` (cond 10) now leaves the covariance unchanged to 2.6e-14 or better, where the old `f tr(C)/K U U^T` deviated by 0.1 to 13. It no longer scales with the current covariance: a float32 test whose content maps grow the spread about 5.5 times per layer lost definiteness by the third layer under the new floor, so that test now uses contracting maps, and the guarantee is exact-arithmetic definiteness, not float32 definiteness under geometric growth. The `predictive_covariance_floor` default stays 1e-4. The LayerNorm covariance pushforward is scaled by `min(1, K max gamma^2 / tr)`, the sphere bound on the exact output, in the plain seam and in the `delta_full` composite, and the capped tokens are counted in `layernorm_trace_cap_elements` (item 1). The prototype diagonal mean field flags a non-finite or non-positive `1 + c D` (item 2). The minimal-agent mean-field label step skips the KL symmetry check and the query-factor autograd; values are bitwise unchanged and gradients agree to float32 rounding, because the dropped log-determinant term is multiplied by a row sum that is zero analytically (item 21). The self-edge double count is documented as a trade-off (item 10).

Item 18 was not changed: composed (token, position) frame rows are nearly all distinct, so there is nothing to deduplicate.

## Frame condition bound cost

With `phi_frame_condition_max = 1e4` training fell from about 10 to 5 iterations per second. The bound decomposed every row of `phi_embed` (V x H blocks) in float64 and ran a 64-step bisection over all of them after every accepted step. It now screens each row in float32 with the exact bound `lambda_max - lambda_min <= sqrt(2) ||S_0||_F` of a traceless spectrum, and decomposes only the flagged rows: in float32 with a residual and orthogonality certificate (`tau = 64 d eps_32`), recomputing a row with an uncertified block in float64. Rows passing the screen are untouched. On CPU at V = 50,257, K = 20, H = 2 and `phi_scale = 0.035` with 200 stretched rows, the call took 15.9 ms against 665 ms for the all-row float64 version; the GPU time was not measured. The flagged rows match the float64 projection to 1e-5.

## Configuration removals (second pass)

At the user's request a second pass removed or hardcoded fields of `VFE3Config` whose alternatives were unused, numerically equivalent or no longer wanted. Four agents worked on disjoint field groups in separate worktrees, and the coordinator merged them. Each removed key is registered in `RETIRED_CONFIG_KEYS`. A saved value that matches the surviving behavior, or that was inert for its configuration, loads silently; a value whose forward this build no longer computes is refused at load.

**Training loop and runtime.** `divergence_abort` is removed: the divergence monitor still records interval statistics, warns on spikes and forces metrics rows, but it never stops a run, and summary.json no longer carries `training_status`. `fill_uninitialized_memory` is hardcoded off, which skips the deterministic NaN fill kernels without changing loss or gradients. `train_window_offset` is hardcoded to the per-epoch random origin; the origin comes from the train loader's generator when it shuffles with one and from the global CPU RNG otherwise, and in that case the saved origin is restored directly on resume. Evaluation windows keep their fixed origin. A checkpoint of a fixed-window run no longer passes the resume data-identity check. `matmul_precision_retry` is hardcoded on, so the replay ladder acts whenever `fp32_matmul_precision` is below "highest" (train steps still excluded under the fp16 GradScaler). `diagnose_agent_elbo` was kept as a toggle after measurement: it runs on every diagnostic forward, including the log-step diagnostics of the minimal agent, and on a K = 16, N = 32 CPU model it adds about 1.5% of training wall time under the mean-field minimal agent (its extra float64 exact readout), 0.4 to 0.6% under exact inference and a negligible amount for prototype agents. All four removed keys are dropped silently on load.

**Decode and numerics.** The expected-likelihood cross-entropy has no kernel selector: CUDA tensors always run the fused Triton kernel, and the chunked torch kernel survives only as the automatic path for CPU tensors; the kernel that ran is still recorded. The full-covariance pair KL is fixed at the former `fp32_escalate_cond` policy (float32, recomputed in float64 when a Cholesky row fails, the certificate rejects an operand or the pivot-ratio trigger fires), and the precision-policy plumbing and `vfe3/families/policy.py` are gone; standalone `FullGaussian` calls, which defaulted to float64, now follow the same path. The so_n irreps are built only in the canonical basis, and the eigh tensor-power construction is deleted; `serialize_config` writes `irrep_basis: "canonical"` into every so_n save as a marker, and an so_n payload with the eigh basis or without the marker is refused, since its learned tables live in the eigh basis. The linear decoder always carries its zero-initialized, weight-decay-free per-vocabulary bias; a linear checkpoint saved without it is refused. `BaselineConfig.decode_bias` is kept, and `matched_to` sets it True.

**Norms, block MLP and attention priors.** The final norm before decoding is removed (formerly `norm_type_final="none"`, which ablations had found better than a final LayerNorm), together with the helpers that served only it in the post-hoc interventions, the logit-lens replay and the gauge record (`final_norm_gauge_equivariant`). Each block applies its norm and then its MLP with no post-MLP renormalization, and under `amp_dtype` the block MLP's mean path always runs in a float32 island. With that island fixed, every block-MLP mode on the minimal-agent route gives bit-identical loss and gradients under CPU bf16 autocast, so the `amp_dtype` inactivity rule there now covers every block-MLP mode. The non-causal priors `uniform`, `alibi` and `windowed` are removed; their causal counterparts `causal`, `causal_alibi` and `causal_windowed` remain, and the training-time causality check is kept as a guard for future registrations. A saved final norm, an enabled post-MLP norm or a removed prior is refused.

**Receiver and self-coupling.** The `ambient_alignment` receiver law is removed. The projected_gradient receiver always scores each message by its channel likelihood in the d-dimensional value space and counts the receiver entropy once, and `relational_receiver_noise` now denotes only the channel floor νI_d. The default receiving measurement is `inverse`, the dual measurement P_h with P_h O'_g = δ_hg I, which is invariant under (U, T, S, O') ↦ (Uh, Th, Sh, h⁻¹O') and whose α → 0 minimizer is the kernel mean; `adjoint` is kept for charts too ill-conditioned for the inverse's float64 certificate, and configs saved before the field existed reload with it through `_HISTORICAL_DEFAULT_PINS`. The constant self-coupling form and `lambda_alpha` are removed. `state_dependent` remains as the frame-invariant scalar form, which is the gauge-pure self-coupling on gaussian_full and gaussian_block (per-coordinate α over ordered conditional KLs is not frame-invariant there), and `state_dependent_per_coord` remains the default; the self-coupling is therefore always profiled, and the `constant_lambda_alpha` pure-path flag is gone. Since `relational_receiver_law` and `relational_receiver_update` were introduced in the same commit, a projected_gradient payload with neither key ran the ambient law and is refused, as is an explicit `ambient_alignment` on that route; a saved `lambda_alpha_mode="constant"` is refused on every route. The test baseline in `tests/profiles.py` moved to the scalar state-dependent form; the receiver tests were re-derived against literal channel-likelihood energies at the snapshot self-coupling, and the projected_gradient entries of `tests/data/equal_head_goldens_20261005.pt` were regenerated on the build that captured them.

**Frame exponential (M5).** On CUDA the frame exponential is a fixed-schedule degree-18 Taylor scaling and squaring (Bader, Blanes and Casas 2019) whose squaring count is chosen on the device and bounded through the fail-closed chart radius, so it never reads the device on the host; its Fréchet-derivative backward follows the same schedule (Al-Mohy and Higham 2009). Forward values agree with `torch.linalg.matrix_exp` to fp32 rounding; gradients are more accurate at large gradient scale. The right and left factors share one batched call. On the RTX 5090, contended by another job, the frame build went from 14 host synchronizations to 2 and became 15 to 45% faster. CPU keeps `torch.linalg.matrix_exp`.

**Precision (item 18).** An RTX 5090 profile of the live route found no operation lowered to bf16 under `amp_dtype="bf16"`. About half of GPU time on the live route is the decoder's elementwise log-sum-exp chain, a candidate for a fused kernel.

**Dead code.** About 1,650 lines unreached from production were removed from `vfe3/` and the multi-seed driver (the metric and numerics-monitor registries, the post-hoc holonomy module, the retraction registry, the vocabulary comparison driver and smaller leftovers), together with the 79 probe scripts of `agent_tooling/`. The sixteen in-repo `importorskip` calls became plain imports.

## LayerNorm trace cap reverted to K·max γ²; source recovery fixed at fp32

The centered trace cap Kλ_max(ΠΓ²Π) of the round-2 remediation (M3) is reverted to the sphere bound K·max γ². The centered bound tightens it by at most K/(K−1), 5% at K = 20, and paid for that with about 64 small kernel launches per norm call for the multisection of its secular equation. The restored bound is `K * weight.square().amax()` without `.detach()`, so the γ gradient stays exact where the cap binds (item 3): the central-difference check at K = 4 in float64 passes against it for both layouts. The multisection solver, its implicit-function gradient and the tests that only checked it were removed; the Monte Carlo test that the output covariance trace stays below the cap now reads K·max γ², and the closed-form diagonal test asserts that the cap does not bind there.

The projected source recovery now has a single working precision, fp32. The setter `set_projected_recovery_precision`, its module default of fp64 and the call in `VFEModel` are gone, so the scorer-primitive tests run the policy production runs. Flagged factors are rebuilt by float32 shifted CholeskyQR3 of the source root; a failed float32 retry, or a float64 input, still takes the float64 QR, and the tests that covered float64 tiles and the commutant rebuild in float64 now reach that path by forcing the retry to report failure. Eight scorer-primitive accuracy assertions written for the fp64 policy were rederived for fp32. On the rank-ill source maps with κ(X) = 10⁴, 10⁵ and 10⁶, the bound |ΔKL| ≤ εκ(r + 2KL + |w|(|μ_i| + |μ_j|)), from a whitened factor error of at most εκ and the float32 means amplified by 1/σ_min, gives 4.9e-3, 4.9e-2 and 0.48 against measured errors of 4.4e-5, 3.8e-4 and 1.4e-2. At κ = 10⁴ the recovered error stays more than two decades below the float64 KL of the rounded float32 Gram (4.8e-2), and at κ = 10⁶, where εκ = 0.12, float32 recovery keeps the score finite but certifies no digits. The gradients on the κ = 10⁴ source fixture are now compared normwise against the first-order bound 6εκ = 7.2e-3, with a measured maximum of 2.8e-4; the elementwise comparison had failed at 2.6e-2 on entries that are small against a gradient spanning twelve decades. The shear fixture has κ(X) = 9.0·10⁶ > 1/ε₃₂, outside the range of float32 CholeskyQR3, and its 2.3e-8 error on a KL of 5e-7 is the float32 assembly of (tr + |w|² − r)/2 at tr ≈ r, bounded by εr/2 = 1.2e-7 and 21 times below the error of the uncertified score. On the factor route the per-pair model of `_factor_status`, ε(κ_q + κ_k)(tr + quad + r), bounds a nearly dependent query root with κ up to 1.3·10⁶ at a measured 0.21 of the bound, with or without the certificate. Under fp32 recovery a certificate-flagged CholeskyQR2 factor is kept, so on the `cholesky_qr2_certified` route the certificate changes no score and only gates acceptance on the float64 fallback; the certificate test now checks the flag itself and that no entry is rebuilt.

## Fused Triton kernels for the KL decode cross-entropy

A profile of the live route (B = 64, N = 128, K = 20, two heads, four layers, the gpt2 vocabulary, `gaussian_full` with `full_chunked`) put about half of the GPU kernel time in the elementwise chain of the chunked KL decode cross-entropy. The new field `decode_ce_triton` (default False, added to the `train_vfe3.py` dictionary and the test baseline, inactive off `diagonal_chunked` and `full_chunked`) runs that chain in two Triton kernels (`vfe3/kernels/kl_decode.py`, `vfe3/kernels/_kl_decode_triton.py`) on CUDA tensors. The cross-term GEMM of each vocabulary chunk stays in cuBLAS under the same IEEE fp32 pin, so the (S, Vc) score matrix is the torch route's. The forward kernel adds the bias, clamps, divides by the temperature with IEEE `div_rn`, adds the unigram term, and carries an online maximum, sum of exponentials and target logit across chunks in three (S,) buffers. The backward kernel recomputes the scores tile by tile, writes dCE/da into one (S, Vc) buffer for the two cuBLAS backward GEMMs, and accumulates the row sums for the per-position term and the temperature. The cross term is kept for backward exactly when the torch route would keep its chunk workspace under `decode_ce_checkpoint`, and is recomputed otherwise. CPU tensors always run the torch loop, and `summary.json` records the kernel that ran as `decode_ce_kernel`. torch.compile of the chunk function was measured as the alternative: 17.1 ms and 2.4 GB for the diagonal decode forward and backward against 46.4 ms eager, after an 18 s first compile, so the Triton route (7 ms) was kept.

The logits are the torch route's bitwise, which the CUDA test checks on the target logit and the row maximum. Three defects of the first version were found and removed. Saving lse = m + log l rounded it to the ulp of the large logits at tau = 0.01 and multiplied the gradient error by up to 20, so m and log l are now kept apart. `tl.exp` (`ex2.approx`) tripled the temperature-gradient error at tau = 1, so the compiled kernels use the libdevice exp. A target of a later chunk that fell on a padded column of a tile was counted as a hit; the interpreter test caught it. Against a float64 CPU reference over sixteen configurations (both decoders, tau 0.01 and 1, sigma_init 1 and 15, untied and tied tables, ignored targets, the unigram term), the two routes have the same CE error and their largest tensor-gradient errors agree within 5 percent (6e-7 at tau = 1; 1e-5 to 4e-5 at tau = 0.01, where both routes lose the same digits). The error of the scalar temperature gradient lies between 4e-9 and 7e-7 on both routes with no consistent order between them (the fused error is 13 times the torch error in one configuration and 6 times smaller in another); the model freezes the temperature. The offline sm_120 compile reports zero spill bytes for both kernels in fp32 and fp64 (44 to 96 registers).

On the RTX 5090 with the device idle, the diagonal decode forward and backward at the live shapes took 7.0 ms instead of 47.7 ms, and the whole training step of the live full-covariance route took 86.5 ms instead of 126.8 ms (median of 18 interleaved steps), with peak allocated memory 3.45 GB instead of 5.72 GB. With the user's training job on the same device, the decode took 18.5 ms instead of 116.2 ms (`full_chunked`) and 16.2 ms instead of 117.4 ms (`diagonal_chunked`), its peak memory fell from 4.17 to 1.90 GB, and the step took 149.8 ms instead of 251.1 ms. Under `decode_ce_checkpoint = "auto_aggregate"` the fused decode recomputes the cross term and peaks at 0.58 GB against 1.61 GB. The tests are in `tests/test_kl_decode_triton_20261012.py`.

## The certified factor route rebuilds its flagged factors

At the user's decision, `cholesky_qr2_certified` now rebuilds every entry whose CholeskyQR2 factor fails the certificate ε₃₂κ̂₁(C) ≤ 3·10⁻⁴, by the same fp32 recovery as `gram_certified`: the source root is formed in float64, rounded to float32 and factored by shifted CholeskyQR3, with the float64 QR when that retry fails. The kept-flagged behavior came from two conditions. In `_strict_cholesky` the working-precision branch returned the prepared factor when no entry had failed outright and rebuilt only the failures on the factor route; both now rebuild failed or flagged entries on either route, and the prepared CholeskyQR2 factor, finite at a flagged entry, is overwritten only there. In `projected_gaussian_kl` the GEMM error estimate kept flagged key entries on the float32 factor route (`keeps_flagged`); they now leave the estimate as they leave the GEMM. Rebuilt entries are counted in `source_recovery_elements`, and their pairs take the triangular-solve tiles. The configuration comment and the docstrings of `_strict_cholesky`, `projected_gaussian_kl` and the factorization ratio in `vfe3/train.py` describe the new behavior.

The rebuilt factor has the same O(εκ) whitened error as the CholeskyQR2 factor it replaces, so the change is not a per-entry accuracy guarantee. On six seeded cases with κ(X) = 3·10³ on one query head, the flagged pairs' mean error against float64 fell in five and rose in one (1.3e-5 to 2.5e-5), and the summed means went from 1.50e-4 to 1.14e-4; on the same construction at κ = 2·10³ and 5·10³ the mean error rose in two of twelve cases, by at most 1.7×, and fell in the rest. Every pair stays within the first-order bound ε(κ_q + κ_k)(tr + quad + r) of `_factor_status`, the rebuilt pairs at most 0.025 of it, and pairs touching no flagged entry are bitwise those of the uncertified route. At float64 the certificate is not evaluated, so the certified call is bitwise the uncertified one. On a two-token case with one flagged query entry (κ = 3.1·10³), the float32 gradients agree with float64 central differences to at most 0.004 of the normwise bound 6εκ = 2.2e-3. The test that pinned "flagged but kept" now requires the rebuilt count to equal the flagged count; new tests in `tests/test_precision_factor_route_20260924.py` cover the rebuild, the float64 case, the gradients, and the unchanged Gram routes. The focused relational, factorization, recovery and configuration files gave 361 passed; the full CPU suite gave 4,775 passed, 20 skipped and one failure, `test_generate_history_figures_writes_each_figure_only_when_its_data_exist`, which finds open matplotlib figures left by an earlier test on the same worker and passes when its file runs alone.

## Certified factor-route rebuilds in float64

The float32 rebuild of the preceding section left a flagged entry with the O(ε₃₂κ) whitened error its certificate had rejected, so at the user's decision `cholesky_qr2_certified` now rebuilds each certificate-flagged entry as the float64 QR factor of its float64 source root, the factorization the float64 fallback already used. In `_strict_cholesky` the factor route under `projected_condition_recovery` (float32 input) skips the working-precision branch and takes the float64 recovery for every rebuilt entry, so the factor tensor is promoted to float64 and the recovery mask marks exactly the rebuilt entries. `projected_gaussian_kl` then scores every pair touching such an entry through its existing float64 factor-route tiles, with the means recomputed in float64 from the source tuple, and rounds the result to float32 only at the end; the other pairs keep the float32 GEMM and are bitwise those of the uncertified route. Entries whose CholeskyQR2 factorization fails outright take the same float64 rebuild on this route, since such a failure already implies ε₃₂κ(X)² ≳ 1 and a float32 rebuild of them would itself fail the certificate. The uncertified factor route keeps its float32 shifted CholeskyQR3 for failures, and `gram` and `gram_certified` are unchanged, the latter keeping its fp32 recovery. The recovery counter counts the rebuilt entries and the GEMM error estimate excludes them, as before. The configuration comment, the module comments and the docstrings of `_strict_cholesky` and `projected_gaussian_kl`, the factorization paragraph of `vfe3/train.py` and the comment in `VFEModel` describe the new behavior.

On twelve cases (seeds 0 to 3 of the mixed fixture with κ(X) = 2·10³, 3·10³ and 5·10³ on one query head, 15 to 25 touched pairs each), the largest touched-pair error against the float64 score fell from 1.2e-4 with the kept CholeskyQR2 factor, and 8.1e-5 with the float32 rebuild, to 2.1e-6 with the float64 rebuild; the summed per-case mean errors were 3.6e-4, 1.9e-4 and 4.6e-6. Every case's maximum is at most 0.083 of its kept-factor maximum, whereas the float32 rebuild raised the maximum in four of the twelve cases and the mean in two. Each float64-rebuilt pair lies within ε₃₂ times the case's largest touched |KL| and within 1.04 ε₃₂ of its own |KL|; the remainder above the float32 rounding of the result is the healthy key partner's float32 factor, with κ_k ≤ 16. On a one-token case whose single flagged query entry (κ = 2.5·10³) is the only ill-conditioned one, the float32 gradients agree with float64 central differences to at most 7.8 ε₃₂ relative per input, 0.28 of the bound 6ε₃₂κ over the entries that keep a float32 factor (κ ≤ 4.6); the S_q gradient, which the flagged entry dominates, falls from 400 ε₃₂ with the kept factor to 0.26 ε₃₂. On the nearly dependent query root (κ up to 1.3·10⁶) the certified route now runs the float64 QR, and its rebuilt pairs fall from 8.3e-3 to 1.3e-6.
