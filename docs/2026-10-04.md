# Changes — October 4, 2026

## Diagnosis of the seed-dependent training slowdown

Two runs of the same launcher configuration (K=64, two heads of dimension 32, four layers, B=32, N=128, diagonal Gaussian, linear decode, projected_predictive with moment-matched covariance and the exponential content chart) differed only in seed. Seed 6 held about 185 ms per step for 30,000 steps, while seed 54 degraded from 186 ms at step 1,000 to 1,140 to 1,270 ms by steps 8,000 to 9,000, and its peak memory jumped from 9.3 to 12.6 GB at step 1,600. Three investigations (a GPU profiler on the saved checkpoints, a code inventory of every float64 path, and a conditioning decomposition of the saved states) agree on the cause.

The cost is the frame-pair float64 escalation in `vfe3/geometry/transport.py`, which commit e573df2 made unconditional on October 2. When any block of a producer call fails the certificate eps_32 kappa_F(U E) > 1e-4, the whole call's frame pair is rebuilt in float64, and through `relational_scoring._compute_dtype` every relational consumer then runs in float64, forward and backward, at the RTX 5090's 1/64 FP64 rate. Forcing the escalation on the healthy seed-6 state reproduces the collapse (218 to 1,517 ms per step), and disabling it on the seed-54 state removes it. The moment-matched covariance update accounts for about 72% of the escalated overhead, because it densifies the block-diagonal frames to K x K.

The trigger is seed specific. Seed 54's learned positional generator at position 0 acquired a strong contracting stretch in the first few hundred steps: kappa_F(exp Y_0) is 445 at step 8,000 against 45 for seed 6, while every other position stays below 8. Composed with the token frame at position 0, it crosses kappa_F = 838.9 for a few tokens per batch, so 61 of 64 seed-54 batches escalated at step 8,000 and 15 of 64 at step 4,500, and seed 6 never did. The same stretch drives the logged projected-covariance conditioning. Position 0 attends only to itself, its moment-matched covariance follows a closed recursion, and the diagonal projection taken in the stretched local frame raises kappa(C) from 3e5 at layer 0 to 4.6e8 at layer 1. Removing the stretch (replacing U_0 by its polar factor, or Y_0 by Y_1) removes every failure in a counterfactual scan of the whole vocabulary. The logged `relational_projected_cov_min_eigenvalue` of -8.2e7 and the condition numbers above 1e7 are float32 `eigvalsh` noise on matrices with lambda_max of 1e15 to 1e17, not genuine indefiniteness.

With the escalation off, the seed-54 failures of the float32 zero-jitter Cholesky (all position-0 query entries) take the always-on source recovery. Its cost is not the float64 QR but the float64 pair tiles that every pair touching a recovered token then uses, about 230 ms per recovering step. Float32 shifted CholeskyQR3 of the source root recovers 100% of those entries with whitened factor error at most 9.1e-4, the same as float32 Householder QR of the same root.

The K=256 slowdown (eight heads of dimension 32) is a different mechanism. At B=16 the moment-matched covariance's dense (B, N, K, K) intermediates push peak memory to 46.3 GB, and the Windows driver spills to shared system memory instead of raising out-of-memory, so a step takes 12 s.

## Toggles

Six fields were added to `VFE3Config`, installed by `VFEModel.__init__` and exposed in `train_vfe3.py`.

`frame_pair_fp64_escalation` (bool, default False) reinstates the opt-in switch that existed from September 23 to October 2. Off, no certificate is formed and the stored pair keeps its inverse residual of at most eps_32 kappa_F(U), about 2e-4 at seed 54's worst frame; `CanonicalFrameContext` still fails closed above min(64 K eps kappa_inf, 1e-2), and its error message now names this field. Measured against a float64-frame forward, the loss moved by at most 9e-5 nats. The default follows the standing rule that computationally extreme paths are opt-in. A saved config carrying the key loads as saved, and one without it (every run saved October 2 to 4) loads False. `frame_pair_condition_tol` (default 1e-4) exposes the certificate threshold, which was the module constant `_FRAME_PAIR_CONDITION_TOL`.

`projected_cov_recovery_precision` ("fp64" | "fp32", default "fp64") selects the working precision of the projected source recovery. "fp64" is the existing behavior. "fp32" rebuilds the flagged factors by float32 shifted CholeskyQR3 of the source root (backward stable for kappa_2(X) up to about 1/eps_32; Fukaya, Kannan, Nakatsukasa, Yamamoto and Yanagisawa, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 42 (2020) A477), keeps the factor tensor in the working dtype, and evaluates the pairs that touch a recovered entry by the same triangular-solve tiles in the working dtype. Only the per-entry source root is still formed in float64. A call whose float32 retry fails falls back to "fp64". On the factor route, certificate-flagged entries keep their CholeskyQR2 factor, since a float32 redo cannot improve on it. The noisy-query cost keeps its float64 pair tiles under either setting.

`projected_gram_condition_tol` (default 1e-4) and `projected_factor_condition_tol` (default 3e-4) expose the two certificate thresholds of `projected_cov_condition_recovery`. The Gram threshold was a literal inside `_cholesky_status`, unreachable by any setter. The factor threshold is written to `relational_scoring._FACTOR_CONDITION_TOL`, the value read at call time. Each is reported by `inactive_fields()` when its route or the recovery is off.

`predictive_covariance_checkpoint` (bool, default False) recomputes the moment-matched covariance update in backward under a non-reentrant checkpoint. Values and gradients are bit-identical (checked on CPU, and by the GPU profiler on both seeds, escalated and not).

`train_vfe3.py` sets the escalation off, the recovery precision to "fp32", the thresholds to their defaults and the checkpoint off at K=64. The existing launcher values, including `projected_factor_route = "cholesky_qr2"` and `projected_cov_condition_recovery = True` from the 12:45 edit, were left as they were. `run_artifacts.py` records the escalation switch and the recovery precision in the pure-path report, and `vfe3/paper/tables.py` lists them among the factorization and exponential keys.

## Accuracy trade on the Gram route

With the escalation off, the Gram route again accepts float32 factors of near-threshold entries. At seed 54 those are the position-0 keys at layer 1 (kappa(G_k) of 5e6 to 1.35e7), whose KL to later queries carries errors up to 1.5 nats and routing-weight errors up to 2.4e-2 against a float64 oracle. The cholesky_qr2 route without the certificate brings these to about 1e-3 nats and 4e-6 in the weights, at about 60% more healthy step time. The effect on the training loss stayed at or below 9e-5 nats in every arm measured.

## Blockwise moment-matched covariance

`RelationalOperators.predictive_covariance_update` now evaluates diagonal beliefs on block-diagonal frames (the diagonal family on block_glk and so_n frames) by a blockwise route; the full family and dense frames keep the dense K x K route, which stays in the code as `_predictive_covariance_update_dense`. With U_i block diagonal and Sigma_i diagonal, C_i = U_i^{-1} Sigma_i U_i^{-T} is block diagonal, the head-pair source term is T_h C_i T_g^T = sum_m T_h^(m) C_{i,m} T_g^(m)T over the d x d blocks, and diag(U_i C_i^att U_i^T) reads only the diagonal blocks of C_i^att, so no K x K intermediate is formed: the dense frame expansion, the diagonal embedding, both K x K frame sandwiches, the off-diagonal blocks of C_i^att and of the residual cross term are all gone. It is the same update reassociated. The new `tests/test_blockwise_moment_matched_20261004.py` checks it against the dense route to 1e-11 in float64 (H from 1 to 4, with and without the residual, batched and unbatched, the stored chart and a generic O'), in float32, and in every input and parameter gradient to 1e-10, and checks the dispatch. The existing `tests/test_predictive_covariance.py` checks both routes against an independent enumeration of the predictive kernel and runs gradcheck through the blockwise route.

Counted with `torch.utils.flop_counter` (forward and backward, N=128, residual on), the update costs 5.4M multiply-adds per token instead of 8.4M at K=64 (H=2) and 149M instead of 415M at K=256 (H=8); a single CPU thread ran the K=256 forward and backward in 0.52 s instead of 1.09 s. The remaining cost is the head-pair source and lift contractions (about K^3 each) and the N K^2 pooling over sources, which this formulation cannot avoid. A GPU A/B was started and, when a training run started on the GPU, stopped at the user's request. The stop did not reach the benchmark's child process: it ran on for 45 seconds, finished the K=64 part and wrote its results after the output file was last read, so this section first reported that it produced no timings. Those K=64 timings were taken while the user's training process shared the GPU and are not a valid measurement: median CUDA step times of 204.5 ms (dense) and 492.6 ms (blockwise) on the seed-6 state, 598.9 ms and 654.2 ms on the seed-54 state, and 445.9 ms for blockwise with the fill disabled. They do not show the speedup the FLOP count predicts, and if the seed-6 pair survived a clean measurement the blockwise route would be the slower one at K=64. The same process compared one seed-54 batch under bf16 autocast: the losses agree to 7.6e-6 and the largest per-parameter relative gradient difference is 0.074, a size consistent with bf16 rounding of two association orders, although the per-parameter breakdown was not recorded. The K=256 part never ran.

## Uninitialized-memory fill

## Evaluation schedule

`train()` also evaluated on a fixed schedule, independent of `eval_interval`. Whenever a run directory was given and `eval_interval >= 1000`, it ran a validation pass before the first update (step 0) and at completed steps 1000, 3000 and 8000 when they fell below the first regular evaluation (`EARLY_EVAL_STEPS` and `EARLY_EVAL_MIN_INTERVAL`, audit 2026-09-28). Each of those passes ran the full evaluation: validation CE, the validation diagnostics, the fixed training-sample CE, the residual-ratio, attention-statistics and cap-activation CSVs and the map snapshot. The positive steps also generated a sample, wrote the attention heatmaps and took part in best-model selection. The step-0 row was held back and written ahead of the first metrics row. This is where the map snapshots at steps 0, 1000, 3000 and 8000, the `attention/step_1000_*` heatmaps and the step-1000 and step-8000 evaluation rows in runs with `eval_interval = 30000` came from. At the user's request that schedule is removed: evaluation now runs only when the completed step count is a multiple of `eval_interval`, and the end-of-run final evaluation is unchanged. The step-0 machinery went with it: the held row, its two flush points and `_group_parameter_norms`, which only that row used.

Nothing downstream needs the removed rows. The S1 log-variance panel took its decay-only starting value from the step-0 `sigma_log_mean` when present and otherwise falls back to `log(sigma_init)`. The table is initialized to that constant, so the value is the same. The map-invariant drifts are measured from the analytic initialization, not from a step-0 snapshot, and the M3 curves already dropped step 0 on their log axes. Runs that already carry step-0 and early rows read as before. The stale "(early evaluations)" remark on `M3_LOG_X` in `vfe3/paper/figures.py` is removed, and its value is unchanged.

Two new tests in `tests/test_training_diagnostics_20260928.py` cover the change. `test_no_validation_before_the_first_eval_interval_step` trains 4 steps with `eval_interval=1000` and checks that metrics.csv holds only the log rows at steps 2 and 4, with no training-sample CE and no map snapshot; before the change it failed with rows `[0, 2, 4]`. `test_long_run_evaluates_only_on_eval_interval_multiples` runs 1500 loop iterations with `eval_interval=1500` and checks that the only evaluation row, map snapshot and training-sample entry are at step 1500; before the change it failed with rows `[0, 1000, 1500]`. Because 1500 real CPU steps take about 15 s, that test replaces the optimizer step with a no-op that reports an accepted step, the only `status_out` key the loop reads. The fixtures that forced an early schedule by patching the removed constants are gone from `tests/test_training_diagnostics_20260928.py`, `tests/test_training_counters_20260928.py` and `tests/test_cuda_zero_device_gates_20260929.py`. The expected schedules in those files are now the `eval_interval` multiples alone, and the three files pass (32 tests). The full CPU fast lane (`not slow and not cuda and not external`, 12 single-thread file shards) recorded 3,043 passed, 12 skipped and 2 failed. The two failures are the chart-radius tests attributed above to the uncommitted `transport_chart_max_norm = 50.0` default: they expect a validity-bound error at frame norms 30 and 21.2, both inside 50. The slow lane was not run.

## Frame pairs at IEEE fp32 under TF32

With `fp32_matmul_precision = "high"` the user hit the `CanonicalFrameContext` contract (max inverse residual 0.0125 against the 0.01 cap at `vfe3/contracts.py:95`), and with `"highest"` did not. The contract compares the stored pair in float64, so the check itself is unaffected by TF32. Each exponential pair is consistent by construction, because its inverse is the float64 inverse of the rounded forward factor. The composed frame `(U E, E^-1 U^-1)` in `build_factored_transport`, however, comes from two separate float32 products, and so does the RoPE fold in `fold_rope_into_frame`. Under `"high"` those GEMMs run as TF32, whose rounding is about 2^-10 instead of 2^-23, and each product rounds on its own. The pair's residual then scales as 2^-10 times the frame's condition. At the condition of seed 54's position-0 frame (kappa_F of about 445) that scale is of order 0.4, so a residual of 0.0125 is expected; at `"highest"` the same quantity is about 5e-5. The frame-pair escalation is not a remedy here: its certificate uses the fp32 epsilon, so it does not see TF32 rounding, and a tolerance low enough to see it would escalate every call.

Both builders now run under `vfe3.runtime.ieee_fp32_matmul`, which pins `"highest"` for their forward GEMMs and restores the caller's policy on every exit, including exceptions. This was the existing `prior_bank._ieee_fp32_matmul`, which protects the a_v interval certificate for the same reason; it moved to `vfe3/runtime.py`, and `prior_bank` now aliases it. Under `"highest"` the context yields immediately, so runs at the default policy are unchanged. Under `"high"` only these small block GEMMs lose TF32; every consumer, and the backward of these products, keeps the caller's policy. The contract's error hint now names the matmul policy when it is not `"highest"`, and keeps the escalation hint otherwise. The new `tests/test_frame_matmul_precision_20261004.py` records the policy seen by every matmul and matrix exponential inside both builders under a global `"high"`, on the compact and dense routes, and checks that the policy is restored, also when the build raises. Before the change those tests saw `"high"`. TF32 does not exist on the CPU, so the GPU effect is not measured here: the confirmation is a rerun of the failing `"high"` configuration. The Gram, factor and CholeskyQR3 certificates of the projected scorer (`vfe3/relational_scoring.py`) also use the fp32 epsilon, so `"high"` weakens them in the same way; that is not addressed by this change and not measured. The full CPU fast lane on the working tree recorded 3,048 passed, 12 skipped and 2 failed. The two failures are the chart-radius tests caused by the uncommitted `transport_chart_max_norm = 50.0` default, as above.

The next `"high"` run (seed 54, K=64, gram route, fp32 recovery) failed at step 468 in `_source_root` with "source covariance must be finite and positive for projected factorization". The variance the projected factorization receives is the previous layer's moment-matched output `diag(U C_att U^T)` with `C = U^-1 Sigma U^-T`, which passes through `U^-1` and back through `U`, so its rounding is amplified by about kappa(U)^2. At TF32 rounding, small output variances can come out nonpositive on ill-conditioned frames, and the recovery correctly refuses them. This mechanism is inferred from the code; it was not reproduced (see below). The same pin now covers every token-linear frame and factorization computation whose accuracy is kappa-sensitive or whose certificate uses the fp32 epsilon:
- the moment-matched update `RelationalOperators._predictive_covariance_update`, on both routes and in its checkpointed recompute;
- `canonical_mean` and the final `U_i body_i` of `predictive_content`;
- `frame_matvec`, `reference_variances` and `reference_covariance`;
- `projected_moments`, `_cholesky_status`, `_factor_status`, `_strict_cholesky`, `_source_root` and the key-precision `cholesky_inverse` of `projected_gaussian_kl`;
- `cholesky_qr2` and `validated_cholesky_solve`.

The O(N^2) pair tiles, the attention pooling, the decode and every backward keep the caller's policy. Two new test files, `tests/test_covariance_matmul_precision_20261004.py` and `tests/test_frame_numerics_precision_20261004.py` (8 tests), record the policy at each pinned operation under a global `"high"`, check that the pair tiles still see `"high"`, and check that the policy is restored; before the change they saw `"high"` throughout. The full CPU fast lane then recorded 3,056 passed, 12 skipped and the same 2 chart-radius failures.
