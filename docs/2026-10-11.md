### Training loop and optimizer

The training step now puts the pre-clip norm of every clip set and the pre-step cross-entropy into the host transfer it already makes, so it costs no extra synchronization (item 1). A host-side monitor uses these values to write, on every metrics row, the following quantities over the steps since the previous row:
- the mean and maximum of the loss and of each role's pre-clip norm;
- the fraction of steps on which the clip was binding;
- the realized clip coefficients.

A role norm above ten times its exponential moving average, or a loss more than one nat above its average, logs a warning and forces a metrics row at that step.

The opt-in `divergence_abort` stops a run after 100 consecutive rejected updates, or when a row's interval-mean cross-entropy exceeds ln V after the warmup. The run then finalizes normally, with `training_status = "diverged"` and the reason in `summary.json`. The scaling point and the per-step wall time of such a run now count the steps it actually ran rather than `max_steps`.

The new `grad_clip_scope = "group"` clips each optimizer group separately, beside the per-role default (item 8). Under the Fisher table mode, the type-prior tables are excluded from clipping (item 9), and the frame step now runs before the table step, so a `fisher_group` metric reads the pre-step rows (item 34). Their AdamW rates are marked inert when nothing else reads them, the logged table rate and step norm are the realized, scheduled values, and two device-to-host reads were removed from the table step (items 20, 37 and 57).

A failed first diagnostic no longer latches the nine-key fallback set and silently drops 52 columns. The log-step diagnostics now run through the same precision-retry ladder as evaluation (item 10). A step skipped for non-finite gradients under a reduced matmul precision is replayed one rung stricter (item 58). The table Fisher docstrings now call the metric a chosen preconditioner, and they state that the trust-radius bound holds only to second order: the exact ratio along +Δl is 1.049 at r = 0.1 and 1.158 at r = 0.3 (items 35 and 36). The decode-mean decay is described as decoupled decay rather than a Gaussian prior (item 43).

### Diagnostics and the canonical free energy

These changes alter only log-step diagnostics; the forward pass, loss and gradients stay bitwise unchanged whether the diagnostics are on or off. Each layer now reports three statistics of the routed covariance (items 3 and 13):
- the projected-covariance scale tr G/r;
- a ratio of the routed belief's covariance to that of the stack entry, which does not read the comparison maps;
- a Gram-normalized smallest eigenvalue of the pencil (P(G), P(N_h)), where N_h = ½(S_Q S_Qᵀ + S_K S_Kᵀ) and P projects onto the commutant of the comparison RoPE torus. The plane normalization uses ¼(‖S_Q[p]‖² + ‖S_K[p]‖²), so an isotropic σ²I reads σ².

The third statistic is invariant along the (S_Q, S_K) orbit that leaves every routing cost unchanged, so a genuine covariance collapse, which lowers all three, can now be told apart from drift of the comparison maps, which lowers only the absolute scale. Each layer also reports the Dong rank-one residual and the identity distance ρ(T̂_hÔ_h − I) of the content self map (item 4). metrics.csv carries the layer minima and the layers that attain them.

A new warning fires when a router reads a covariance below 1e-3 of the stack entry's. The dead-router warning now checks three signals under every clamp reference (item 7):
- self-lock, worded as softmax saturation;
- fully clamped rows;
- a capped-entry fraction above 0.9.

Under these rules the live row would have warned.

The canonical router β* now carries comparison-space RoPE through each head's reference moments. It reports its mean diagonal weight as `canonical_self_weight` (item 18) and uses the router's own temperature under the minimal agent (item 31). It is also evaluated at each layer's converged output, which gives `canonical_total_delta`, the change in F_l across the layer (item 27).

`canonical_routing_rows` now propagates NaN from a failed factorization instead of reporting zeros (item 38). `inactive_fields` no longer marks the diagnostic's inputs inert while the diagnostic is on (item 31). On head-block groups the diagnostic's float64 exponentials run per head (item 53): 9.4 ms and 4 MB per window on the CPU against 56.2 ms and 16 MB dense, at N = K = 128 with four heads, agreeing to 6.7e-16.

### Scorer and numerics

The pair-KL GEMM now runs its backward at IEEE fp32 as well as its forward (item 2). A new autograd function saves only the token-linear inputs and differentiates a recomputation of the expansion inside `ieee_fp32_matmul`. On the CPU it is bit-identical to plain autograd, and a dispatch-level check confirms the backward GEMMs run at "highest" under a "high" policy.

When `rope_comparison_covariance = "rotation_invariant"` leaves at most one comparison coordinate unrotated, as in the active configuration, P(G) is diagonal and the Gaussian KL takes a closed-form diagonal route (item 30). That route forms diag(G) as a sum of squares and uses one GEMM of inner dimension 2r, pinned to IEEE fp32 in both directions, with no Gram matrix, factorization or pair-difference tensor. Measured against the dense route on the CPU:
- in float64 it agrees with the dense route and with a direct-difference reference to about 5e-16, in values and in every gradient;
- in float32 at B = 4, N = 128, H = 4 and r = 32 the two routes differ by at most 6.5e-7 relative;
- forward plus backward takes 0.072 s against 0.130 s.

The remaining scorer items:
- Under the same option with a dense non-rotating block, a failed factorization is rebuilt from the source instead of raising (item 12).
- The inverse receiver measurement is built from the chart factors, T e^{−OT} or T(I + OT)⁻¹, with a certified inverse on the affine chart (item 14). At cond(T) = 1e12 it is accurate to 6e-16 against a 60-digit reference, where inverting O' gave 6.5e-6.
- The zero-variance source root is now differentiable (item 40).
- Non-finite chart blocks reach `matrix_exp` as NaN (item 41).
- Raise-only scorer verdicts are deferred (item 55).
- The Gram tolerance is read under the rotation-invariant option (item 32).
- A one-time warning reports that this option makes the router covariance diagonal; 6 of 16 planes per head turn by less than 0.5 rad at the live settings (item 48).
- The diagonal-family floor docstring now states the weaker guarantee fτ/max_h‖D_h^{-1/2}U_h‖²₂ (item 39); the floor itself is unchanged.

### Block MLP, LayerNorm pushforward and prototype agents

The LayerNorm covariance pushforward now carries the second-order variance along the radial direction, where the Jacobian's eigenvalue ε/(v + ε) had left only the floor (item 11). The added term is ρ(γ∘z)(γ∘z)ᵀ with ρ = tr(H̃ΣH̃Σ)/(2K²s⁴), applied in the full, head-block and diagonal forms and carried through the MLP Jacobian under `delta_full`. Against 400,000 Monte Carlo samples at K = 6 it predicted a radial variance of 2.018e-6 against a measured 2.043e-6; first order alone gives 7.1e-13. At K = 16 the two-token KL now converges as the floor shrinks: 1.59e4, 2.67e4 and 2.69e4 at f = 1e-2, 1e-4 and 1e-6, against 3.63e4, 3.54e6 and 3.54e8 before. A `delta_full` composite, or a block MLP after the pushed norm, keeps a smaller 1/f dependence along γ⁻¹ on `gaussian_full`, which this change does not remove. Because 102.97 used the pushforward on the diagonal family, reloading it now applies the radial term, which that run never trained with.

The full-layout pushforward is now an O(K²) rank-two update. It agrees with the dense route to 1.7e-15 at K = 128 and keeps its Cholesky certificate, applied per head block for `gaussian_block` (item 50). The diagonal closed form selects its float64 recompute without a host read (item 57). A LayerNorm final norm now pushes the covariance too (item 49). In reference coordinates, both pushforwards and a `delta_full` coordinate MLP act on C = U⁻¹ΣU⁻ᵀ and map back covariantly; validation now admits that combination on `gaussian_full` and `gaussian_block` (item 51).

The prototype EM term now takes its expectations under the posterior that produced its weights (item 15):
- under exact inference, the component posteriors, whose gradient matches the central-difference gradient of the log evidence and whose fixed point is λ = V − c;
- under mean field, the last CAVI sweep's moments.

`block_mlp_autocast = False` runs the block MLP's mean path in fp32 under bf16 autocast (item 29). The prototype block reports a mean-field label staleness (item 54). The fused Triton energy accumulates in blocks of 16 features (item 56). In a float32 emulation at τ = 0.1 this lowered the per-row cross-entropy error from 3.6e-3 to 1.1e-3 at K = 512. The kernel itself was not executed, and its spill check was not rerun.

## The K = 128 instability is the KL decoder reading the stack covariance

The K = 128 runs that blew up during training (losses of thousands to millions of nats between steps 150 and 2,500, pre-clip norms up to 1e7) were traced on the GPU with a scratch harness. The harness ran `train()` for a fixed number of steps on the same seed, the same shuffled 20M-token slice of the WikiText-103 training stream and a run's own `config.json`. Run under the 7bfdf27 code, the source the healthy 36.41 run executed, the diverging configuration also diverged: its first losses agreed with the current code to about 2e-6, and its blow-up started near step 200. The instability is therefore not a regression from the October 10 fixes. Within 600 steps, `routing_kl_max_reference="row_min"` brought it forward to steps 150 to 300 under either `rope_comparison_covariance`, and `"absolute"` removed that early window. Over 2,500 steps, however, the `"absolute"` configuration still produced 14 steps with loss above 10 (the largest 9.5 million at step 1,737), reproducing the user's launcher runs. The user observed that the linear decoder trains cleanly.

The decisive comparisons kept that 2,500-step configuration and changed only the covariance the prior-bank decoder reads. With the decoder reading a constant Σ_q = 5 while the router kept the moment-matched covariance, no step exceeded a loss of 10 and the loss over the last 250 steps averaged 4.74. With `predictive_covariance="passthrough"` the corresponding figures were no step above 10 and 4.78. Detaching the decoder's gradient into Σ_q while still reading it made the run diverge at step 105 and stay above 1e18, so that gradient is stabilizing, not the cause. The diagonal KL logit −KL(q‖p_v)/τ contains tr(Σ_v⁻¹Σ_q)/(2τ), which differs across the vocabulary once the decode variances σ_v differ. Every jump of the moment-matched covariance that the six residual-free layers build therefore reaches the cross-entropy, while the linear readout never reads Σ_q.

A new field, `decode_covariance`, selects that covariance: `"belief"` (default, the stack's final Σ_q, bitwise unchanged), `"encoded"` (the token's encoded covariance, the stack's input) or `"constant"` (σ_init I). It is wired through the training seam in `VFEModel.forward` and the inference seam in `forward_beliefs`, so evaluation and generation read the same covariance, and it is marked inert under the linear decoder. The pure-path report gains `decode_reads_belief_covariance` and records the field. Analysis code that decodes captured beliefs directly cannot supply the encoded covariance and refuses `"encoded"`. New tests are in `tests/test_decode_covariance_20261011.py`. The focused CPU run (that file with the config-isolation, decode-mode, prior-bank and projected-decode tests) passed 51 tests. The new field itself was not run on the GPU; its `"constant"` setting reproduces the harness's constant-covariance arm.
