# October 15, 2026

## Negative moment-matched variances at K=160, and a float64 escalation toggle

The K=160 `block_glk` runs (`vfe3_runs/20261014-214527_...` and `20261015-091444_...`) stopped with "projected covariance must be finite" even under `predictive_covariance_ceiling = 1000`. A temporary dump at the comparison check, removed afterward, reproduced the crash on the live config three times (steps 488, 978 in the original run, and 1826; the step varies between runs). In every case the projected covariance G was NaN for one token. Its local input variance was negative, −35.7 in the first repro and −5164 in the third, while the means, the frame inverse (max |U⁻¹| about 22), the score maps and S U⁻¹ were finite and moderate. The diagonal comparison route builds G from the square root of the variance, so a negative variance becomes NaN.

With `n_e_steps = 1`, no full-link E-step, and passthrough norm and block MLP covariances, the layer's input covariance is the previous layer's kernel-readout moment-matched update. A check at `diag(U C_att U^T)` in `_predictive_covariance_update` showed the −5164 there, against an absolute term sum of 6.06e6 and a largest C_att diagonal of 9.2e11. Recomputing only that last contraction in float64 from the same float32 C_att still gave −1679, so C_att itself loses positive definiteness in float32. The update leaves reference coordinates through U, which amplifies float32 rounding by about κ(U)². The 2026-10-04 IEEE-fp32 pin removed the TF32 part of this error but cannot remove float32 cancellation at this scale. The ceiling clamps only from above and the floor adds f Σ_p ≈ 5e-4, so neither can correct the sign.

`predictive_covariance_fp64_escalation` (False by default, in `train_vfe3.py` next to `predictive_covariance_checkpoint`) adds the remedy as a toggle. With it on, `RelationalOperators.predictive_covariance_update` checks the floored float32 output with one host read per call: every variance finite and positive in the diagonal family, a Cholesky factorization in the full and head-block families. A failing call is recomputed whole in float64 and cast back, so backward runs through the float64 recompute, and every token of the call is counted in `source_recovery_elements`. A float64 failure raises `ValueError` without repair. The ceiling is applied after the check. The toggle is reported inert under passthrough covariance, outside `projected_predictive`, and under the minimal-agent readout, which has its own float64 escalation.

`tests/test_predictive_covariance_fp64_escalation_20261015.py` (6 cases, tiny CPU shapes) injects a nonpositive float32 variance. It checks that the update is unchanged with the toggle off, that the escalated output equals the float64 update and is counted, that a healthy update is not escalated, that a float64 failure raises, that gradients reach the float32 leaves, and that the config field is a validated bool. The focused files pass (75 tests). The CPU lane has 6 failures unrelated to this change: they read the uncommitted launcher values (`decode_ce_triton`, `decode_logits_amp`, the removed `kl_max` key) and the `pos_phi_matrix_norm_*` health columns. No training run has been made with the toggle on, so whether the float64 recompute stays positive on the K=160 run, and its cost there, are not yet measured.

## Spike attribution

A divergence spike line named only the role (mu, sigma or phi), and the forced metrics row of a spike step left every `grad_norm_group_*` column blank, because the per-group norms are collected only on log steps. The phi spikes of the K=160 run (step 162: phi 16.4x its EMA together with a loss spike of 1.96 nats) therefore could not be traced to the token frame table, the positional frames or a layer.

On a spike step the training loop now attributes the gradient (`_spike_attribution` in `vfe3/train.py`). The gradients are still live after `train_step`, which zeroes them on entry; `train_step` now also reports each parameter's clip coefficient (`status_out["clip_scales"]`), so the attribution divides it back out and works with pre-clip norms. For each spiking role (every role when only the loss spiked) it reports the role's pre-clip norm, the share of the squared norm in each named parameter group, the five largest parameters with their share, and, for the token tables (`mu_embed`, `sigma_log_embed`, `phi_embed`) and `pos_phi` among them, the three rows with the largest gradient, their share of the table's squared gradient and, for token tables, how often each token occurs in the batch. The listed causes are the coincidences it can establish: a loss spike on the same step (a hard batch), a binding clip, a matmul-precision replay of the step, one parameter carrying at least half of the role's squared norm, a few rows carrying at least half of a table's, and any group more than 10x its norm at the last log step. These are coincidences, not a causal proof.

The result is one more warning line (`step N: spike attribution: ...`), a JSON record per reported spike in `diagnostics/divergence_spikes.jsonl`, and the pre-clip group norms in the forced row's `grad_norm_group_*` columns. A spike whose update was rejected has no gradients left and says so. The work runs only on spike steps, which the monitor reports at most once per 100 steps, and costs one host transfer of the parameter norms. `tests/test_divergence_spike_attribution_20261015.py` (4 cases, tiny CPU model) checks that the attributed role norms equal the pre-clip role norms with and without a binding clip, that the group shares sum to one, that the batch counts of reported token rows are exact, and that a spike step logs the line, writes the record and fills the group columns. The CPU lane shows the same 6 failures as before, from the uncommitted launcher values.

## Escalation by failing sequence

With `predictive_covariance_fp64_escalation` on, the K=160 run fell from about 4 to about 1 it/s after roughly 1000 steps, because every escalation recomputed the whole layer call in float64, and float64 runs at a small fraction of the float32 rate on the RTX 5090. The same run then crashed: its later layers pass the receiving maps O' as a per-run list, and the escalation called `.double()` on the list. Both are fixed in `RelationalOperators._escalate_failed_sequences`. The float64 path converts each run's map, and it recomputes only the sequences that contain a failing token. A receiver's update reads only its own sequence (beta is (B, H, N, N)), so the other sequences keep their float32 values and graph bit for bit. A frame with its own batch axis is split with the beliefs; a shared frame broadcasts. Only the tokens of recomputed sequences are counted in `source_recovery_elements`. Calls without a single leading batch axis, or with edge links, still recompute whole. Two tests were added to `tests/test_predictive_covariance_fp64_escalation_20261015.py`: one passes the receiving maps as a list, and one checks that the healthy sequences are bitwise unchanged with a batched and with a shared frame.

The 36.41 K=128 run's archived source shows the moment-matched kernel unchanged since then except for the floor (f (tr C/K) I in reference coordinates then, f Sigma_p now; f = 1e-4 either way). Its `relational_projected_cov_scale` fell from 1.7e3 to about 200 over 120k steps, while the 2026-10-15 K=160 run's rose from 383 to 1.96e5 by step 800, with the moment-matched covariance pinned at the 1000 Sigma_p ceiling from step 400 on.

A per-layer probe of the live config (1000 steps each, `block_norm_covariance` passthrough and pushforward, same seed) located the inflation. Under passthrough the block MLP returns means of rms about 700 to 2.6e4 from LayerNorm outputs of rms about 0.7, the next block norm divides them back, and the covariance stays in the pre-norm units; the median tr(Sigma)/tr(Sigma_p) out of layer 1 went 1.27, 4.8, 132, 863 at steps 100, 200, 300, 1000, and 90 to 96 percent of tokens at layers 2 and 3 sat at the 1000 Sigma_p ceiling, with about 54,800 escalated tokens by step 1000. Under pushforward no update escalated in 1000 steps and the step time stayed at about 0.2 s, but the covariance collapsed instead: the norm divides Sigma by the same factor of up to 2e4 squared, so the median ratio out of layers 2 and 3 fell to the floor (3e-4 and 1e-4). In both cases the block MLP's mean gain is not matched by its passthrough covariance (`block_mlp_covariance = "passthrough"`), which is where the units come apart. The 36.41 run's mean scales were not measured, so whether its MLP gain was smaller is unknown.

## Why the K=160 configuration diverges and the 36.41 run did not

The 36.41 K=128 run's config, migrated with `config_from_serialized` and run on the current code for 600 steps (same seed, the same per-layer probe), kept the median tr(Sigma)/tr(Sigma_p) between 0.8 and 2.5 at every layer, escalated nothing and reached a training loss of 6.2. The divergence is therefore not a code change. The moment-matched kernel differs from the archived source only in its floor, and the block MLP diff touches only the delta_full and pushforward paths.

Starting from that config, the live settings were applied in groups, for 300 to 400 steps each on one seed, with block-norm covariance passthrough and no ceiling. `decode_covariance = "encoded"` alone made the covariance grow steadily layer by layer (last layer 1.47 to 15.7 by step 400) while the loss stayed healthy (5.64): the decoder then reads the encoded covariance, so nothing in the loss restrains the belief covariance. Weight decay 0 with per-group clipping, and comparison-invariant RoPE at base 100, changed nothing. The Fisher table update gave one mild loss spike. Three groups destabilized training and drove the mean entering layer 1 to rms 40 to 500: the learning rates (a loss of 126 at step 200), K=160 with five heads (10.8 at step 300), and `kl_max`, `decode_tau = 0.05`, the inverse measurement and certified factorization together (14.5 at step 300, with 883k factorization recoveries and 0.75 s steps). With large means the routed spread term enlarges the covariance, and with the encoded decoder nothing pulls it back, so in the full live config it rises to the ceiling.

Switching the live config to `decode_covariance = "belief"` does not cure it. The training loss reached 1892 by step 300, the means entering layers 1 to 3 reached rms 500 to 1700, and half the tokens sat at the ceiling by step 500, so the belief decoder exposes the inflated covariance to the loss (scaled by 1/(2 tau) = 10 at tau = 0.05) instead of preventing it. The primary defect of the live configuration is the training instability that blows up the means; the encoded decoder is what lets the covariance follow without a loss penalty. These are single-seed short runs, so the group attributions are indications, not established effect sizes.
